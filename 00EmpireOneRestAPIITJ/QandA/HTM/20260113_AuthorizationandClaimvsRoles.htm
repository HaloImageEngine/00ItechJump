In ASP.NET Core, authorization answers “is this authenticated user allowed to do this action/resource?” and the most maintainable way to express that is with authorization policies. A policy is a named set of requirements (claims, roles, custom rules, authentication scheme, etc.) that you register in AddAuthorization and then apply with [Authorize(Policy = "PolicyName")] (or per-endpoint configuration). Policies let you centralize rules like “must have a paid subscription,” “must be in the Admin role,” or “must have a claim permission=orders.read,” and they scale much better than scattering role checks throughout controllers.

Claims vs Roles: a claim is a general key/value piece of identity data about the user (e.g., sub=123, email=..., department=Sales, permission=orders.read). Claims are flexible and are commonly used for fine-grained authorization (permissions, features, tenant, subscription, etc.). A role is a special kind of claim used for coarse-grained grouping (e.g., Admin, Manager) and is typically checked with [Authorize(Roles="Admin")] or RequireRole("Admin"). Under the hood, roles are often represented as claims (the “role claim type” can vary), but conceptually: roles are broad buckets, while claims are more granular and can represent many dimensions of access. A common best practice is use roles for high-level access (Admin vs User) and claims/policies for specific permissions (CanRefund, CanEditOrders), often implemented as RequireClaim("permission","orders.write") or a custom requirement/handler when logic is more complex (e.g., tenant isolation or resource ownership).

Summary

Authorization policies are reusable, centralized rules you apply to endpoints; roles are coarse-grained access groups (often implemented as role-claims), while claims are flexible key/value identity attributes ideal for fine-grained permissions.
